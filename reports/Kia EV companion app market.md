# Win Kia owners where Kia's app stops

As of September 2026, **no polished, paid, App Store-distributed third-party companion app for Kia/Hyundai EVs serves UK/EU owners**. The field is split between a broad but clunky official Kia App, free open-source projects (Home Assistant's `kia_uvo`, the TestFlight-only BetterBlue), and single-purpose tools for OBD, routing and charging that don't connect to each other. The user's app would be the first to combine remote control, Octopus Agile-aware charging, cost tracking and OBD battery-health history, and that combination is a real gap. The weakness is its foundation. The unofficial Kia EU API has broken roughly once a year or more since 2024. It enforces a per-account daily cap of about 200 calls. Its use by third-party apps is explicitly prohibited by the Kia Connect Terms of Use. It also exposes the app to Apple's Guideline 5.2.2, which Apple has already applied to unofficial Tesla apps. There is no affordable official alternative for commands: Smartcar's self-serve plan allows only 100 commands a month across a whole fleet, and the EU Data Act route is read-only and does not reach UK cars. The recommendation is to market the app as an independent **"smart charging and battery companion for E-GMP EVs"**. Premium value should come from features that work without the Kia API (Agile planning, cost ledger, OBD battery history, route planning), with remote control as an optional free layer. Price it at **£3.99/month or £34.99/year with a 14–30 day trial**. Don't offer a lifetime subscription while API access is uncertain.

## Kia's official app is broad, free for seven years, and weak on UX

The EU/UK **Kia App** (Kia Corporation, iOS id6740517042) replaced Kia Connect and Kia Charge with one app. It covers remote climate, locks, windows, charging and schedules, a Digital Key, an EV route planner and an Apple Watch app ([Kia UK](https://www.kia.com/uk/electric-hybrid-cars/technology/kia-app/)). It holds **4.1/5 from 4,000+ ratings** on the UK App Store ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/kia-app/id6740517042)). Some newer features need the ccNC infotainment system ("from EV3 onwards"), so early EV6s get only a subset ([Kia UK](https://www.kia.com/uk/electric-hybrid-cars/technology/kia-app/)). Connectivity is **free for 7 years from first sale**, and paid "Kia Connect Premium" packages become available from **May 2026**. Prices vary by model and are shown only inside the app's store ([Kia Connect subscriptions](https://connect.kia.com/eu/product-information/ccs-subscriptions/); [Kia Connect FAQ](https://connect.kia.com/eu/customer-support/faq/)). Owners are therefore used to getting connectivity at no cost. That is the main reason a third-party app cannot charge for remote buttons alone.

Owner sentiment is worse than the star rating suggests. App Store reviewers call the redesign "too busy". Forum threads, readable only as snippets behind a paywall, say the charge-limit setting is "buried" under a sprocket icon, report repeated logouts, and describe EV6s that forget off-peak charging settings ([kiaevforums](https://www.kiaevforums.com/threads/new-kia-app-is-worse-than-last.15007/); [SpeakEV](https://www.speakev.com/threads/new-kia-app.191243/page-2)). Remote climate is widely described as "hit and miss" ([Kia Owners Club](https://www.kiaownersclub.co.uk/threads/climate-control-from-the-kia-connect-app.73724/)). Hyundai's Bluelink Europe does worse still, at about **1.8/5 from ~18k Android ratings** ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/hyundai-bluelink-europe/com.hyundai.bluelink.eu.ux20)). The research found no evidence that the Kia App offers home-screen widgets, Live Activities or Control Center controls. That is absence of evidence rather than proof, so check the current build before relying on it in marketing.

The biggest competitive change is recent. In **August 2026 Hyundai Motor Group named Kaluza its global smart-charging partner**. Tariff-aware charging is being built into the Kia App, with **OVO as the first UK supplier from autumn 2026** and V2G planned from 2027 ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/08/27/hyundai-group-integrates-smart-charging-control-from-kaluza/); [Kaluza](https://www.kaluza.com/press-releases/kaluza-hyundai-motor-group-global-ev-charging-partnership)). This closes the smart-charging gap for OVO customers only. Octopus customers are left out, and it is not yet clear whether 2021–23 EV6s are covered.

Sources for the table: [BetterBlue](https://markschmidt.io/betterblue.html), [kia_uvo on GitHub](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/kia_uvo), [HA analytics](https://analytics.home-assistant.io/custom_integrations.json), [TRONITY pricing](https://www.tronity.io/en/tronity-app/pricing), [Car Scanner App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/car-scanner-elm-obd2/id1259933623), [ABRP App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/a-better-routeplanner-abrp/id1490860521), [EV Tariff](https://evtariff.co.uk/blog/intelligent-octopus-go-compatible-cars/).

The open-source numbers show real demand. `hyundai_kia_connect_api` has 368 stars and `kia_uvo` has 941 stars and **6,034 opted-in installs**, which undercounts real use ([GitHub](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/hyundai_kia_connect_api); [HA analytics](https://analytics.home-assistant.io/custom_integrations.json)). But these tools are for tinkerers, and **both core repositories are looking for new maintainers** ([GitHub kia_uvo](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/kia_uvo)). BetterBlue is the closest match to the user's iOS feature set. It is free, needs TestFlight, and appears to be built for the US first ([GitHub BetterBlue](https://github.com/schmidtwmark/BetterBlue)). Its existence means widgets and a Watch app cannot be sold as premium features.

Smart charging is the clearest unmet need. Kia and Hyundai E-GMP cars **have no car-side integration with Intelligent Octopus Go**, because HMG "has not opened the API surface required for third-party schedule control" ([EV Tariff, Jul 2026](https://evtariff.co.uk/blog/intelligent-octopus-go-compatible-cars/)). Octopus supported Kia at one point and then removed it. Owners attribute the removal to polling that **drained the EV6's 12V battery** ([Kia Owners Club](https://www.kiaownersclub.co.uk/threads/kia-has-been-removed-from-the-octopus-intelligent.73526/)). OVO's car-side route through Enode is described by OVO itself as "extremely unreliable for reasons beyond OVO's control" ([OVO Forum](https://forum.ovoenergy.com/electric-vehicles-166/charge-anytime-with-kia-19500)). Today the practical integration point is a smart charger such as Ohme, which handles Agile natively ([EV Tariff Ohme review](https://evtariff.co.uk/chargers/smart/ohme-home-pro-review/)).

This cuts both ways. An EV6 owner on Agile without a smart charger has no product that serves them. But the reason no product exists is that polling the car API is fragile. An Agile planner that works (set the car's schedule and charge limit once per night, rather than repeatedly waking the car) would be a real differentiator. A naive one would repeat Octopus's failure.

On OBD, the market is commoditised. **Car Scanner's Pro unlock costs £7.99 once** and the app is rated 4.8/5 from ~9,800 ratings. Its E-GMP profile reads SOH, cell voltages and temperatures ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/car-scanner-elm-obd2/id1259933623); [OBDadvisor](https://obdadvisor.com/obd2-scanner-electric-car/)). If a buyer or seller needs proof of battery health, the route is **Aviloo's £100 TÜV-certified test**. From July 2026 it comes with a purchase guarantee in the UK, though sources disagree on the payout (£2,700 or £3,000) ([Aviloo](https://aviloo.com/en/); [electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/06/16/e3000-for-battery-defects-aviloo-converts-battery-diagnostics-into-a-purchase-guarantee/)). A one-off OBD read is not worth paying for. A **long-term SOH trend history tied to the car's charging record** is not offered by any current iOS app, and it would help when the car is sold.

## The unofficial API fails, caps usage, and breaches Kia's terms

Kia's EU backend has broken the community libraries repeatedly:

- **August 2024**: a daily cap arrived, with a server message that blamed "3rd party apps" ([kia_uvo #918](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/kia_uvo/issues/918)).
- **August 2025**: the token format changed ([#855](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/hyundai_kia_connect_api/issues/855)).
- **September 2025**: Kia EU login began requiring a Google reCAPTCHA. For a time users had to paste browser-extracted refresh tokens ([Kia Europe login wiki](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/hyundai_kia_connect_api/wiki/Kia-Europe-Login-Flow)).
- **October 2025 to May 2026**: a Hyundai EU backend migration broke Hyundai logins for about seven months, and problems returned for some accounts in **August 2026** ([Discussion #921](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/hyundai_kia_connect_api/discussions/921)).

Plan for about one major EU auth break a year, with outages lasting from days to months.

The rate limit is commonly cited as **~200 calls per account per day**. That figure comes from secondary sources, because the original bluelinky wiki page is gone ([kia_uvo #636](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/kia_uvo/issues/636)). Hitting the cap also locks the owner out of the official app for the rest of the day. That is why `kia_uvo` defaults to reading cached data every 30 minutes, forcing a fresh car poll only every 4 hours, and never forcing one overnight, both to protect the 12V battery and to stay inside the quota ([GitHub kia_uvo](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/kia_uvo)).

Kia's terms are explicit. The Kia Connect Terms of Use (Europe) §6.4.2 bar owners from sharing credentials or "us[ing] your login credentials to connect any third-party service" to Kia's back-end. §5.1 bars use "for any public or commercial purpose without our prior permission", and §5.2 reserves all trademarks and logos ([Kia Connect ToU](https://connect.kia.com/content/dam/kia-uvo/global/assets/legal-docs/Kia%20Connect_Terms%20of%20Use_CC_Europe-English.pdf)). The research found **no cease-and-desist letters or account bans** from Kia against third-party apps. Kia tolerates the practice for now, but it is not permitted.

Other carmakers show how this can end. **BMW blocked third-party requests to its servers on 29 September 2025**, which broke Home Assistant and energy providers alike and pushed users onto BMW's official CarData API ([HA alert](https://alerts.home-assistant.io/alerts/bmw_connected_drive/)). Tesla switched off its Owner API in favour of a paid Fleet API. Tessie estimated the cost at **~$60M/year** and Teslascope said the fees would be about **7x its monthly revenue** ([Carscoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2024/11/tesla-api-costs-go-live-and-could-kill-many-apps-one-dev-says-he-faces-60m-bill/)).

Apple has already acted on this category. In **August 2020 it rejected an update to "Watch app for Tesla" under Guideline 5.2.2** and demanded written consent from Tesla, then let the update through while it reviewed its policy ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2020/08/27/apple-rejects-watch-for-tesla-app-as-it-starts-requiring-written-consent-for-third-party-api-use/)). Later developers report requests for OEM authorisation under 5.2.1 ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/717083)). Enforcement is inconsistent. TRONITY, which uses some "un-managed" OEM APIs, is live on the App Store ([TRONITY](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tronity/id1549509183)). Even so, any update could be the one that gets rejected. Removing Kia branding reduces the 5.2.1 (IP) exposure. It does not remove the 5.2.2 (authorisation) exposure.

## Official routes cover telemetry, but none makes commands affordable

| Route | UK coverage | Commands? | Cost | Fit for this app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smartcar | Yes (Kia "all models that support Kia Connect") | Lock/unlock, charge start; climate unconfirmed for Kia EU | Build: from **$1.99/vehicle/month**, **100 commands/month shared across all vehicles**; Enterprise needs 500+ vehicles | Command allowance far too small for preconditioning |
| High Mobility (Kia's official data partner since Jan 2024) | EEA + UK, EV6 included | None found | **€99/month minimum**, per-vehicle price not published | Read-only telemetry for B2B |
| Enode | UK/EU | Charging-focused | Sales contact only | Same backend; had its own Kia/Hyundai error-rate incidents |
| Kia EU Data Act "Vehicle Data API" | EU; UK unconfirmed | No (data only) | Free for owners; "reasonable compensation" may be charged to third parties | Read-only; CCS 1.0 cars update only at end of drive |

Sources: [Smartcar pricing](https://smartcar.com/pricing), [Smartcar Kia](https://smartcar.com/brand/kia), [High Mobility](https://www.high-mobility.com/blog/kia-live-vehicle-data-now-available-via-high-mobility), [High Mobility pricing](https://www.high-mobility.com/pricing), [Enode status](https://status.enode.com/incidents/01JBZ4X4RK1CCFDQAVGQX4D84Y), [Kia Data Act notice](https://www.kia.com/content/dam/kwcms/kme/pl/pl/assets/contents/EU_Data_Act_2025.pdf), [community analysis](https://github.com/Hyundai-Kia-Connect/hyundai_kia_connect_api/discussions/887).

The EU Data Act has applied since **12 September 2025**. It gives owners free access to "readily available" data such as battery level and DTCs, and lets them direct that data to third parties ([EC guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidance-vehicle-data-accompanying-data-act)). Remote locking is classed as a "related service" ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/the-eu-data-act-has-taken-effect-focus-on-automotive-and-cloud-providers)), but nothing found requires OEMs to open command APIs. Kia's implementation handles consent in the app and appears to be data-only. Kia's notice says that on CCS 1.0 vehicles, data updates only at the end of a drive ([Kia Data Act notice](https://www.kia.com/content/dam/kwcms/kme/pl/pl/assets/contents/EU_Data_Act_2025.pdf)). An early EV6 is likely a CCS 1.0 car, but this is unverified.

A UK owner is probably outside the Act altogether. The UK's **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** only gives the government powers to create future "smart data" schemes, and no automotive scheme exists yet ([UK Parliament](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/3825); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/smart-data-schemes-enhanced-data-sharing-in-the-uk-under-the-new-data-use-and-access-act)).

The economics settle the question. At £3.99/month, Smartcar's $1.99 per vehicle takes about 40% of gross revenue before Apple's cut. Even then the Build plan allows only 100 commands a month across all users, which a single user's winter preconditioning could exhaust. Official routes are worth adopting for read-only features, where they are a way to get past App Review. They cannot replace the unofficial API for remote climate at hobbyist or indie scale.

Across these apps the pattern is consistent. Remote controls and basic status are free, because the OEM app already provides them. Paid tiers are built on **automatic charge and drive logging with time-of-use costs, battery degradation and phantom-drain tracking, automations, and alerts** ([Tessie](https://tessie.com/pricing); [Roamer](https://rivianroamer.com/plus); [Teslascope](https://teslascope.com/membership)). Non-Tesla apps charge about half of Tessie Pro. A Facebook VW group asking whether "$4.99 per month [is] worth it" for ABRP suggests where non-Tesla owners start to hesitate ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/666960357264469/posts/1467156883911475/)).

Two cautions apply to how the price is structured. First, RevenueCat's 2026 data puts trial-to-paid conversion at **42.5% for 17–32 day trials against 25.5% for trials of 4 days or less**. Hard paywalls convert **10.7%** of downloads against **2.1%** for freemium. **72% of annual subscribers cancel within the first year** ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Second, when Stats moved lifetime buyers onto a subscription, forums reacted badly ([Cybertruck Owners Club](https://www.cybertruckownersclub.com/forum/threads/%E2%80%9Cstats%E2%80%9D-now-forcing-subscription-on-early-adopters.31969/page-2)). A lifetime tier sold on top of an API that Kia can switch off is a promise the developer may be unable to keep.

## Recommended positioning, features and pricing

**Positioning.** Present the app as an independent **smart-charging and battery companion for E-GMP EVs** (EV6, Ioniq 5/6, EV9). Don't present it as a replacement Kia remote. Choose a neutral name and icon. Refer to cars only in compatibility text ("works with Kia and Hyundai EVs"), and state clearly that the app is not affiliated with Kia. The pitch has three parts: "charge on Agile without a smart charger, see what every mile costs, and watch your battery's health over years." Target Octopus Agile and Go customers who don't have an IOG-compatible charger first. The Kaluza/OVO integration doesn't serve them, and no charger-free product does. Support Ioniq 5/6 as soon as possible, because the library already covers Hyundai EU and the market roughly doubles.

**Architecture as risk hedge.** Keep all credentials and API calls on the device, with no developer server relaying Kia traffic. Poll within `kia_uvo`-style limits and show a "calls used today" counter. Write the Agile plan to the car as one schedule and charge limit per night. Most important, make sure **every paid feature still works if the Kia API disappears or App Review removes it**. The Octopus tariff, OBD SOH history, cost ledger (from OBD, manual entries or charger data) and route planner don't depend on Kia. The Kia connection can then ship as a module that can be switched off without killing the product. Add official read-only sources (the Data Act API in the EU, High Mobility or Smartcar) where they give App Review an authorisation chain for telemetry.

**Free tier.** Remote climate, lock and charge, status, one widget, a Control Center control and the Watch app. These match the official app and the free BetterBlue, so charging for them would drive users away. They are how users discover the app.

**Premium tier ("Plus").** Agile and Go charge planner with a projected cost. Automatic cost ledger (pence per mile, home against public). OBD battery-health history with cell-delta trends and a PDF export for resale. Automation rules. Charging and climate Live Activities. Alerts (charge interrupted, SoC reached, 12V warnings). Route planning with the car's live SoC. CSV export.

**Price.** **£3.99/month or £34.99/year**, with a **14–30 day full trial** and no card required, matching TeslaFi, TRONITY and Roamer and the conversion data above. That is below ABRP (£39.99) and Tessie Basic (£49.99+), which fits an app that has to justify itself next to a free official one. **Hold off on a lifetime subscription.** If buyers ask for a one-off purchase, offer a **one-off "Battery Report" unlock (about £7.99, Car Scanner's price)** for the OBD features. Those features don't depend on the Kia API, so the promise can be kept. Consider a higher **"Business" tier (about £7.99/month)** later, with HMRC-style mileage and home-charging reimbursement reports. TRONITY charges about 3x its consumer price for its tax logbook ([TRONITY](https://www.tronity.io/en/tronity-app/pricing)).

**Watch points.** Track the Kaluza rollout: if it expands to Octopus, the main premium feature loses much of its value. Watch for any Kia enforcement similar to BMW's, and for App Review asking for OEM authorisation. Watch whether Kia opens schedule control to partners. Check whether the Kia App ships widgets or Live Activities. From 2028–29, early EV6s leave the free 7-year window. Owners who don't renew Kia Connect lose remote features entirely, and an OBD-only mode keeps them as customers.

## Conclusion

The opportunity is not in remote buttons. Kia gives those away and so does open source. It lies in the parts of the EV6 owner's experience that nobody connects: the tariff, the charger-free schedule, the running cost and the battery's long-term health. The evidence also turns the usual advice around. The feature that makes the app distinctive, car-side Agile charging, depends on the least reliable part of its stack. So the product has to be built so that losing the Kia API is a degraded mode rather than a shutdown.

Two open questions deserve checking before any launch spend. The first is whether the user's 2022 EV6 is a CCS 1.0 car, which decides whether official Data Act telemetry could ever be useful for it. The second is whether a UK owner can reach Kia's Vehicle Data API at all. If both answers are unfavourable, the unofficial API stays the only route to live data. That supports the recommendation to price the app low, take it slowly, and put its value in features Kia cannot switch off.
